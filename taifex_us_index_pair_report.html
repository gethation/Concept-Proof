UDF / SPF / UNF / SXF against their US legs · methodology mirrors the CCF-UMC study · generated 2026-08-03 03:28

Verdict. Of the four, only UNF (Nasdaq-100) has enough liquidity to carry a pair strategy at all, and even it does not clear its costs. The basis is real and it does converge — but the whole edge is about 5.5% a year with a completely free order book, on the same capital convention that gives CCF-UMC roughly 89%.

The decisive number is not the spread, it is the size of the dislocation. TAIFEX and CME price the same index within a few basis points of each other, so there is simply not much to harvest. SPF, UDF and SXF fail earlier and harder, on trade frequency and on structure.

1. The contracts

All four are quanto futures: they pay a fixed NT$ per index point regardless of USD/TWD. Two consequences. There is no FX term in the spread — both legs are directly comparable in index points, unlike CCF-UMC which needs USDTWD. And fair value carries a quanto/rate-differential term that decays to zero at expiry, which is why this study pairs matched expiries (TAIFEX Sep vs CME Sep): the carry cancels and there is no roll discontinuity.

3. The basis itself

UNF sits about 9.5 bps above NQ on average. Naive quanto fair value would put it below, by roughly the TWD-USD rate differential over the remaining life; the persistent premium is local demand, and it is a level, not a signal. The rolling z-score absorbs it.

Why trade prices lie here

Measured on trade prints, the first-order autocorrelation of basis changes is -0.53 (UNF), -0.53 (UDF), -0.51 (SXF) in the night session. Roll's model puts pure bid-ask bounce at exactly -0.5, and the basis level itself has autocorrelation near zero — it is white noise. A z-score backtest on those prices produces an excellent-looking Sharpe that means "buy at the bid, sell at the ask", which is the one thing a taker cannot do.

The day session is different: basis autocorrelation runs 0.60 at one minute and is still 0.37 at thirty, and change-autocorrelation is -0.38 rather than -0.5. That is a genuinely persistent basis with bounce on top — which is why this study uses the day session and hedges with CME futures, which trade nearly around the clock.

5. The one measurement that is genuinely unresolved

Two ways of measuring UNF's day-session spread disagree, and the break-even sits between them:

  • 9.3 bps — the daily report's last best bid/ask. Real quotes, but only one per session, and market makers widen into the close.
  • 0.7-3.0 bps — Roll's estimator on trades that print close together in time, which isolates bounce. But it measures what people who did trade achieved, and they were patient; a signal-driven strategy is closer to the quoted number.

Break-even is about 6.0 bps, so the two measurements bracket it. That gap can only be closed with intraday quote data, which neither TAIFEX's free downloads nor IB provide — IB carries no TAIFEX products at all. It matters less than it looks, though: even at zero cost the edge is 5.5% a year. Resolving the spread decides between a small loss and a small gain, not between failure and a business.

6. The other three

SPF (S&P 500) — no market

4 contracts a day in the day session and a median quoted spread in the hundreds of basis points. Over the year, 54% of day sessions had no two-sided quote at all. Nothing to study.

UDF (Dow) — too few moments to trade

Over the same sample the day session produced only 8.5 prints per session. The zero-cost grid best is 7 trades for 6,612 TWD (0.31% on NT$2,100,000, about 1.4% annualised) — and that is before any cost at all.

SXF (SOX) — structurally unhedgeable in the day session

There is no listed SOX future anywhere, and the SOX index and every semiconductor ETF are closed during TAIFEX day hours. So SXF can only be paired during the night session, where its quoted spread is 78.3 bps. The hedge itself is also unsound: SOXX has tracked the ICE Semiconductor Index rather than PHLX SOX since 2021, and SMH tracks MVIS 25 — measured against SXF, SMH shows a level correlation of 0.82 and a 160-minute basis half-life, which is tracking error, not a converging basis. A true hedge means executing the ~30 SOX constituents.

7. Reproducing this

IB carries no TAIFEX products, so the Taiwan leg comes from TAIFEX's own tick archive and the US leg from IB Gateway.

python scripts/build_qff1_1m.py --product UNF --expiry-rule third_friday \
    --from-cache --contract-month 202609 --out data/processed/unf_202609_1m.csv
python scripts/download_ib_us_index_legs.py --symbols NQ --expiry 20260918 \
    --bar-size "1 min" --start 2026-05-06
python scripts/calculate_us_index_basis.py --product UNF \
    --taifex-path data/processed/unf_202609_1m.csv \
    --us-path data/processed/ib_nqu6_1m_taipei.csv --session day --tag unf_nq_day

TAIFEX publishes only the last 30 trading days, so --from-cache replaying the archived zips under data/raw/taifex_time_sales is the only route to longer history — keep archiving them. The basis file defaults to event time (one row per TAIFEX print) rather than a clock grid: about 85% of day-session minutes have no UNF print, and a stale leg against a live one drifts apart mechanically, which both invents fills and inflates the sigma the z-score is measured against.
